LHO Fellows Program – A Summer at Hanford

Created in August 16, 2026 by Rachel Langgin

2026   ·   LIGO   Gravitational-Waves   research   ·   lho-fellows

Two Months In

It’s hard to comprehend two months have flown by since I first arrived at LHO in June. In that time, I’ve attended a regional conference, presented at collaboration meetings, gone on a public tour with legendary Fred Raab, explored more of southeastern Washington, and licked the concrete encasing the X-arm beam tube. That last one has been a joke back home since before I even got here, but little did everyone know I was very serious about this task.

This won't be in the official fellowship report. "I'm going to lick the LIGO arm" was basically my catchphrase before I left. Two months in, I made good on it. 10/10, would recommend.

Read more and check out my recent images below.

HAM7 Day

One of my favorite days this summer was the chance to work in the laser vacuum and equipment area (LVEA) and getting hands-on with HAM7 (Horizontal Access Module 7). HAM7 houses the quantum squeezer and its crystal, which converts one higher-energy photon into two lower-energy photons that produce squeezed light. We are able to get more sensitivity at higher frequencies using this technique and surpass the quantum noise limit. You can read more about the quantum squeezer here.

Working in-chamber meant I got to wear “full-bunny” clean room garb to protect the detector from our human contaminants. Once suited, I got to assist staff scientist Camilla Compton with aligning some laser beams. We used laser alignment cards and my iPhone camera to see the near infrared and green lasers. I only spent a couple of hours adjusting the quantum squeezer paths which was still a lot of hard work in layers of garb. Still, I’m glad I got the chance to see one of the optics tables up close before we closed the chamber doors!

A day in the LVEA: HAM7 optics and layout, chasing green and red beams across alignment cards, and a control room work session to close it out.

Outside the LVEA, I’ve also found my favorite seat in the control room.

Proof that the control room yoga ball is the best chair option.

GWANW: Hosting the GW Explorer Tutorial

A highlight of the last few weeks was the Gravitational Wave Astrophysics in the Northwest (GWANW) meeting, held right here at LIGO Hanford Observatory. I presented my home research on Early Warning GWs and hosted a tutorial built around my GW Explorer project (read about the project: GW Explorer Workshop at Las Vegas Academy). This was a fantastic opportunity to have GW researchers use the tutorials. I got immense positive feedback from the participants and I’m even more excited to see this project grow beyond the Las Vegas bubble that it was constructed in. I even had lots of interest in new contributors to the project and desire to translate the project into more languages!

Hosting the GW Explorer tutorial at GWANW.

A Saturday Tour with Fred Raab

I tagged along on one of the First Saturday Public Tours, led by Fred Raab, who’s been with LIGO since the very beginning. Getting the history from someone who was actually there isn’t something I take for granted, so I showed up on a Saturday for this one. Fred told us about the installation and what’s been learned after 20 years of running. My favorite detail: the beam tubes were insulated when they went in, but after years of operating at Livingston, the insulation started failing. Turned out rodents had been chewing through it, and crews had to go down the 4km arms pulling the insulation out by hand. An instrument that can measure changes smaller than a fraction of a proton, taken down by rats. Every time I join a tour of the site, I learn and notice new details I hadn’t before.

Visiting the beam tube, the retired winding stairs from the corner station, a closer look at the test mass excitation setup at the LExC, and capping the day off with a LIGO star party under the Summer Triangle.

Weekend Adventures

My favorite weekends have been exploring the Tri-Cities and beyond — the Pasco Farmers Market on a Saturday morning, and a drive out to Walla Walla for wine tasting with a few people from the observatory. We have also been to so many cafes it is hard to count. The fellows and operators went to Art in the Park around Richland where there were so many local artist booths and food trucks.

A mocha from Ethos, a piñacolada from art in the park, and me in a tree I could not resist climbing.

One of the most exciting nights was the Boating Derby where another fellow and I watched car and lawn-mower racing, and closed the night with boats getting dragged by cars smashing into each other. I’ve never seen or been apart of something so American. There was also a LIGO BBQ where I painted some astronomy-inspired magnets and brought my famous buffalo chicken dip.

Daniel Sigg's cat, outtakes from the boat derby, LEGO LIGO, and my hand-painted magnets from the LIGO BBQ.

Two months in out of my six month stay and this experience has already been so fulfilling. As I am writing this, we just locked the Y-arm of the interferometer after pumping down the corner station for the past month. I’m really excited to be here as we continue commissioning the detector and move into the upcoming Observing Run in November. Thanks for reading and check back in soon for more updates!
